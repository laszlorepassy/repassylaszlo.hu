Breathing exercises (not just) for exam season

Two simple breathing exercises for calming exam nerves and regaining focus.

The tension caused by a test or a final exam is perfectly natural. By consciously guiding our breathing, we can effectively influence the state of our nervous system. The exercises below help you regain your focus and reduce stress.

Box breathing

You can use this breathing exercise any time you feel nervous or want to concentrate better. Imagine that the rhythm of your breath is drawing a square. Breathing in, holding, breathing out and the pause after breathing out all take the same amount of time. Sit, stand or walk comfortably, with a straight back, while you practise.

The exercise

  • Breathe in for 4 counts: 1, 2, 3, 4.
  • Hold it: 1, 2, 3, 4.
  • Breathe out: 1, 2, 3, 4.
  • Rest: 1, 2, 3, 4.

That was the first square. Now comes the second one.

  • Breathe in again.
  • Hold it for just as long.
  • Breathe out for just as long.
  • Keep it out for just as long.

Carry on breathing like this 8 to 10 times in all. If your thoughts wander in the meantime, bring your attention back to your breath.

Finishing

When you stop the exercise, let your breathing return to its natural rhythm. Notice how you feel.

Watching the breath

An easy, restful breathing exercise you can use at any time. Sit, stand or walk comfortably while you do it.

Start paying attention to your breathing. Where do you feel it most? In your nose? In your chest? In your belly? Notice what it feels like as the air flows in… and flows out. You do not have to change it, you do not have to force it. Just watch your breath as it is.

If your thoughts wander, that is fine too. When you notice it, simply bring your attention back to the next in-breath… and then to the out-breath.

Keep watching for a few more breaths: in… and out. Notice the flow becoming calmer and slower.

You can watch your breath any time you want to settle down or feel better.
